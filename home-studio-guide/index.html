A preview — from Chapter 1: Planning Your Space

The following is a short excerpt from the guide.

Before you spend a dollar on gear, spend an hour thinking about your room. The single biggest factor in how your recordings sound isn't your microphone, your interface, or your monitors — it's your room acoustics. A $50 microphone in a well-treated room will outperform a $500 microphone in an untreated bedroom every time.

Choosing the Right Room

If you have a choice of rooms, here's what to look for:

  • Irregular shapes are better than perfect squares or rectangles. Square rooms create strong standing waves that colour your sound badly.
  • Carpeted floors absorb mid and high frequencies that would otherwise bounce around and muddy your recordings.
  • A room with existing furniture — bookshelves, sofas, curtains — already has some natural acoustic treatment built in.
💡 The Clap Test
Stand in the centre of your room and clap once, sharply. If you hear a flutter or ringing that decays slowly, your room has problematic reflections. If the clap sounds dull and absorbed quickly, your room is well-damped.

Basic DIY Acoustic Treatment

You don't need an acoustic engineer or expensive panels to make a meaningful improvement. The goal isn't a dead, anechoic room — it's controlling the worst reflections while keeping enough life in the space to sound natural.
